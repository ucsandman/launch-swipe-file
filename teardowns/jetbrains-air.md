# JetBrains Air: AI can produce code, organizations still have to produce software

## The launch
On September 22, 2026, JetBrains CEO Kirill Skrygan introduced JetBrains Air on the company blog: an open system of products for agentic software development spanning the IDE, team workflows, and organizational governance. The system bundles three products: Air in JetBrains IDEs (an agentic experience inside the IDE), Air Teams (coordinating developers and autonomous agents), and Air Governance (the renamed JetBrains Central, for policy, audit, and AI spend). JetBrains is shipping it as a rolling series of releases, not a single launch day.
Source: https://blog.jetbrains.com/blog/2026/09/22/introducing-jetbrains-air/

## What they did
- Led with a sentence that reframed the whole category: "AI can produce code. Organizations still have to produce software. Agentic development is changing how software gets made, but it hasn't changed what it costs to be wrong." Nearly every outlet quoted it verbatim.
- Bet openly on a multi-vendor future instead of only their own agent: Junie works across all the Air surfaces, and rival agents connect through the Agent Client Protocol. JetBrains positioned itself above the model wars rather than inside them.
- Made the umbrella the launch: Air names six months of existing experiments (Central CLI, shared context, cloud agents, automations, governance, AI cost controls) plus new ones, shipped as a rolling series with explicit "available now vs preview vs longer-term direction" labeling.
- Wrote the post as the CEO, personally: Skrygan described JetBrains as "going through the same transition as the organizations we build for, adopting agents internally, redesigning workflows, and learning where individual productivity gains translate into better software delivery."
- Held the line on the IDE while expanding past it: "For 26 years, we have focused primarily on the individual developer workbench. Now, we are building for the wider system through which agentic work is initiated, executed, coordinated, reviewed, and governed."

## What worked
- The CEO's personal byline plus the "most significant steps in our 26-year history" framing gave press a news hook bigger than a feature: InfoWorld, TechGig, IT Europa, and RuntimeWire all ran coverage within days.

- The Alpha plugin for IntelliJ-based IDEs gave developers something to install on day one while the rest of the system ships over time.

## Takeaways for LegCli
- One quotable sentence about the cost of being wrong beat every feature list. Write the sentence first, then build the launch around it.
- A rolling release series turns one launch into months of beats. Each release can carry its own "what works now, what is preview, what is direction" note.
- If your rivals will eventually live inside your system, say so loudly. Multi-vendor openness is the enterprise trust play.
- A founder-authored announcement (Skrygan, not a press team) carries more weight than a press release ever will.
