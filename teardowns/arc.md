# Arc: the waitlist as a launch engine

## The launch
Arc, from The Browser Company, launched as an invite-only Mac browser in 2022 and opened to everyone with Arc 1.0 in July 2023. The Windows beta followed in December 2023. The pitch: not a browser but "an operating system for the internet," with a rethought interface (sidebar spaces, command bar, site customization).
Sources: https://www.engadget.com/the-arc-personalized-web-browser-is-now-available-to-everyone-140015020.html and https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/11/arc-browser-launches-its-windows-client-in-beta/

## What they did
- Ran invite-only access for over a year, turning every invite into a social object people posted about.
- Built the waitlist in public: 500,000+ on the Windows waitlist at beta, over 1 million by the full Windows release.
  Source: https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/the-browser-company-releases-arc-for-windows/
- Announced each expansion (Mac GA, Windows beta, Windows GA) as its own launch moment with its own press cycle.
- Paired the company with the product story: founders talking openly about the build (Swift on Windows, design decisions) kept the X and tech-press conversation alive between launches.
- Raised $50M at a $550M valuation mid-launch-cycle, which funded the long runway a waitlist strategy needs.
  Source: https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/the-browser-company-releases-arc-for-windows/

## What worked
- A million-person waitlist for a browser, a category everyone considered dead. Scarcity plus a genuinely different product created sustained hype across multiple years.
- Each platform expansion re-ignited coverage without needing a new product.

## Takeaways for LegCli
- If you cannot serve everyone on day one, make the constraint the story: a waitlist with public numbers is a launch asset.
- Stage your rollout (Mac, then Windows; or CLI, then teams) so each stage gets its own press beat.
- Founders narrating the build in public keeps the launch warm for months. The product is the event; the building is the series.
