# Cursor: the biggest swing sentence wins

## The launch
Cursor (Anysphere) launched as an AI-first code editor, a fork of VS Code extended with LLMs. The founding story: four MIT students building "a magical tool that will one day write all the world's code." Their $60M Series A brought in a16z, Thrive, OpenAI, Jeff Dean, Noam Brown, and the founders of Stripe, GitHub, Ramp, and Perplexity.
Source: https://readwrite.com/what-is-cursor-ai-coding-app/

## What they did
- Opened with the largest possible ambition stated plainly. "Write all the world's code" is absurd and memorable, which is why it traveled.
- Let a quotable analogy do the explaining: CEO Michael Truell called it "Google Docs for programmers." One image, zero jargon.
- Recruited credibility through the cap table: when the founders of Stripe and GitHub back your dev tool, every engineer notices.
- Let usage lead: engineers at AI startups adopted it early and talked about it, so the launch kept re-launching itself through word of mouth.

## What worked
- Over 30,000 customers across enterprises, research labs, and startups, per their Series A announcement; ~$400M valuation at the time.
  Source: https://readwrite.com/what-is-cursor-ai-coding-app/
- Became the default reference point for the entire AI coding category; every competitor launch is now framed as "versus Cursor."

## Takeaways for LegCli
- Write the biggest true sentence about your product and lead with it. Timid positioning gets timid coverage.
- Find your "Google Docs for X" analogy before launch day. If you cannot explain it in one image, the press will explain it wrong.
- Engineer word of mouth deliberately: get the tool into the hands of people whose peers copy their setups.
